=== pupil_blur_demo: when is pupil-imaging blur a concern? ===
DM 96x96 at 1.00 mm pitch; influence 1/e 0.85 mm; map grid 449 x 0.250 mm (the actuator sites are grid nodes); 2852 lit actuators (0.85 x 0.74 R_ap)
actuator Nyquist = 0.500 cyc/mm; kernel lambda = 0.05 (of the stencil peak); read noise = 20 pm rms per map pixel (16 pixels per actuator)
error = rms(recovered - true) over the lit actuators, piston removed, / rms(true commands)
APPROXIMATIONS (say them to a skeptic): the blur is applied to the PHASE map -- the small-phase limit (the 30 nm surface is ~0.1 wave of
  wavefront); the camera blurs INTENSITY, and the four-step phase of blurred fringes equals the blurred phase only near null, which holds here.
  The built leg is not a Gaussian: it is a phase gain cos(phi) with amplitude cross-talk sin(phi) plus a distortion; a Gaussian is matched to it
  at the actuator-Nyquist MTF only.  The lit set is the demo's 2852 actuators (0.85 x 0.74 R_ap); the benches light 5072 (lens) / 6948 (mirror)
  from the traced cone, and the redo pupilsim decks fill the DM (the DM is the stop) -- the error is a per-actuator rms, so the count sets the
  edge-to-interior ratio, not the scale.

THE CROSS-CHECK (Fang's question): the blur the built leg would cost, ignored, on the random 30 nm surface, vs the deck's raw-map pupil-imaging share:
  lens   Gaussian at the leg's Nyquist MTF: 0.0040% (map) / 0.0082% (naive command read)  vs the deck's 0.13%  -> ratio 0.031  ** MORE THAN 3x: a FINDING, not a rounding
  mirror Gaussian at the leg's Nyquist MTF: 0.0242% (map) / 0.0494% (naive command read)  vs the deck's 0.29%  -> ratio 0.084  ** MORE THAN 3x: a FINDING, not a rounding
  a Gaussian would need a 1/e radius of 0.036 mm (lens) / 0.054 mm (mirror) to cost the deck's shares -- inside the deck's stated 0.05-0.14 mm, i.e. the deck's
  width and the records' Nyquist gain line describe DIFFERENT things: a gain 0.9994-0.9999 at Nyquist is a 1/e radius of 0.006-0.016 pitch, not 0.05.
  What a gain line cannot show is error OUT of phase with the surface -- a registration residual.  The records' recovered single pokes are shifted
  lens   by up to 0.0004 mm; the random surface shifted by that much costs 0.0474% (map metric) = 0.36 of the deck's share
  mirror by up to 0.0001 mm; the random surface shifted by that much costs 0.0119% (map metric) = 0.041 of the deck's share
  read: the leg's Nyquist MTF alone accounts for 3% (lens) / 8% (mirror) of the deck's share; the rest is what the Gaussian does not model.
  The records' error by band also puts the mirror's largest share in the LOWEST band (0-0.06 cyc/mm: 0.50% of the surface there), which no blur produces.
  The demo's answer to "is blur a concern": no -- at the built leg's MTF the blur costs 0.004% / 0.024% even uncalibrated; the deck's share is an upper
  bound on the pupil imaging that carries more than blur.
